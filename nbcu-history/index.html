A century of entertainment, innovation, and global media dominance

1912

Universal Pictures Founded

Carl Laemmle founds Universal Film Manufacturing Company, making it one of the oldest surviving Hollywood studios.

Universal was founded on April 30, 1912 by Carl Laemmle, Mark Dintenfass, Charles Baumann, Adam Kessel, Pat Powers, William Swanson, David Horsley, Robert H. Cochrane, and Jules Brulatour. The studio opened Universal City in 1915, a 230-acre facility in the San Fernando Valley that remains the world's largest film and television production facility.

1926

NBC is Born

The National Broadcasting Company launches as the first major broadcast network in the United States, created by RCA.

NBC was formed by the Radio Corporation of America (RCA) on November 15, 1926. It began with two semi-independent networks: the Red Network (entertainment) and the Blue Network (news). The Blue Network was later sold in 1943 and became ABC. NBC's first broadcast was a star-studded gala from the Waldorf Astoria Hotel in New York City.

1941

NBC Television Begins

NBC launches commercial television broadcasting, becoming one of the first TV networks in the world.

On July 1, 1941, NBC's New York station WNBT (now WNBC) began commercial television broadcasting with the first officially licensed commercial during a Dodgers game. The station broadcast to a limited audience of about 4,000 TV sets in the New York area.

1953

Color Television Pioneer

NBC broadcasts the first coast-to-coast color TV broadcast, revolutionizing the television industry forever.

NBC was at the forefront of color television, with parent company RCA developing the compatible color TV system. The famous NBC Peacock logo was created in 1956 specifically to showcase color broadcasting to viewers with color TV sets. The peacock's six colorful feathers demonstrated that a show was being broadcast "in living color."

1956

The Peacock Logo Debuts

NBC introduces its iconic peacock logo with six colorful feathers to promote color TV programming.

The original NBC peacock featured eleven feathers in different colors. It was designed by John J. Graham to highlight NBC's color broadcasts. The logo went through several redesigns, with the current six-feathered version adopted in 1986. Each feather represents one of NBC's six divisions at the time.

1975

Jaws Changes Cinema

Universal releases Jaws, directed by Steven Spielberg, creating the modern summer blockbuster phenomenon.

Jaws became the first film to earn over $100 million at the domestic box office, fundamentally changing how Hollywood marketed and distributed films. Universal pioneered the wide release strategy with a massive TV advertising campaign. This single film created the concept of the "summer blockbuster" and established Universal as a major force in cinema.

1986

GE Acquires NBC

General Electric purchases RCA and its subsidiary NBC for $6.4 billion, beginning a new corporate era.

GE's acquisition of RCA (and thereby NBC) for $6.4 billion was one of the largest corporate mergers of the 1980s. Ironically, GE had been one of the original founders of RCA in 1919 but was forced to divest its stake in 1930 due to antitrust concerns. The acquisition reunited the two companies after 56 years.

1989

Sky Television Launches

Rupert Murdoch launches Sky Television in the UK, pioneering satellite broadcasting in Europe.

Sky Television was launched on February 5, 1989 by Rupert Murdoch's News International. It offered four channels via the Astra satellite: Sky Channel, Sky News, Sky Movies, and Eurosport. The service competed directly with British Satellite Broadcasting (BSB), which launched a year later. Both services hemorrhaged money in a fierce battle for subscribers.

1990

BSkyB is Formed

Sky Television and British Satellite Broadcasting merge to form BSkyB, ending the satellite TV war.

After both companies faced massive financial losses, Sky Television and BSB merged on November 2, 1990 to form British Sky Broadcasting (BSkyB). Though technically a merger, it was widely seen as a takeover by Sky, with Murdoch retaining control. The combined company went on to dominate UK pay television for decades.

1990

Universal Studios Japan Announced

MCA/Universal expands its theme park empire with plans for international parks.

Following the success of Universal Studios Florida (opened 1990), Universal began expanding its theme park business internationally. The company developed Universal Studios Japan, which opened in Osaka in 2001, and later Universal Studios Beijing (2021). Today, Universal Parks & Resorts is one of the world's largest theme park operators.

1994

Must See TV Era

NBC dominates primetime with "Must See TV" Thursday nights featuring Friends, Seinfeld, and ER.

NBC's Thursday night lineup became a cultural phenomenon in the 1990s. The "Must See TV" branding, launched in 1993, packaged shows like Seinfeld, Friends, Frasier, and ER into appointment viewing. At its peak, NBC's Thursday night was the most profitable night in television history, with 30-second ad spots during these shows commanding premium prices.

2004

NBCUniversal is Created

NBC and Vivendi Universal Entertainment merge, combining broadcast and film powerhouses into one entity.

On May 12, 2004, GE's NBC merged with Vivendi Universal Entertainment to form NBCUniversal. The deal created a media conglomerate spanning broadcast television, cable networks, film production, and theme parks. GE held 80% ownership while Vivendi retained 20%. This merger united the NBC broadcast network with Universal Studios and its vast content library.

2011

Comcast Takes Control

Comcast acquires 51% of NBCUniversal from GE for $13.75 billion, reshaping the media landscape.

After an extensive regulatory review, the FCC approved Comcast's acquisition of a majority stake in NBCUniversal on January 18, 2011. The deal was valued at approximately $30 billion. In 2013, Comcast purchased the remaining 49% from GE for $16.7 billion, making NBCUniversal a wholly owned subsidiary. This transformed Comcast from a cable provider into a media-entertainment giant.

2014

BSkyB Becomes Sky

BSkyB acquires Sky Italia and Sky Deutschland, rebranding the combined entity simply as "Sky."

In November 2014, BSkyB completed acquisitions of Sky Italia and Sky Deutschland from 21st Century Fox for approximately $9 billion, unifying the three Sky-branded companies under a single corporate entity. The new pan-European company, simply called Sky, served over 20 million customers across the UK, Ireland, Germany, Austria, and Italy.

2018

Comcast Acquires Sky

Comcast wins a dramatic bidding war against Disney/Fox, acquiring Sky for $39 billion.

In September 2018, Comcast won a tense blind auction for Sky, outbidding 21st Century Fox (backed by Disney) with a bid of $39 billion ($22.57 per share). The acquisition gave Comcast/NBCUniversal a massive European footprint with 23 million subscribers. It was the largest ever European media deal and strategically positioned NBCUniversal as a truly global media company.

2020

Peacock Launches

NBCUniversal enters the streaming wars with Peacock, a multi-tiered streaming service.

Peacock launched on July 15, 2020, initially to Comcast Xfinity customers before expanding nationwide. Named after NBC's iconic peacock logo, the service offered three tiers: a free ad-supported tier, Peacock Premium ($4.99/month), and Peacock Premium Plus ($9.99/month, ad-free). It launched with a library of over 20,000 hours of content including classic NBC shows, Universal films, and original programming.

2021

Tokyo Olympics on Peacock

Peacock streams the Tokyo Olympics, providing over 7,000 hours of live coverage to millions.

The 2021 Tokyo Olympics (delayed from 2020 due to COVID-19) became a major showcase for Peacock. The platform streamed over 7,000 hours of live coverage, making it the most comprehensive Olympic streaming experience ever offered. The Games attracted 5.7 billion streaming minutes and helped Peacock gain significant subscriber momentum.

2022

WWE Comes to Peacock

Peacock becomes the exclusive streaming home for WWE content in the U.S., adding 3+ million subscribers.

In March 2021, WWE Network migrated exclusively to Peacock in the U.S. under a five-year deal reportedly worth over $1 billion. This brought WWE's passionate fanbase to Peacock, including live pay-per-view events like WrestleMania and all of WWE's vast library. By 2022, Peacock had surpassed 30 million monthly active accounts, with WWE being a major driver.

2024

Paris Olympics & Growth

Peacock streams the Paris 2024 Olympics and surpasses 36 million subscribers, reaching profitability.

The Paris 2024 Olympics became Peacock's biggest event, with record-breaking streaming numbers. Peacock reached 36 million paid subscribers by Q3 2024 and achieved profitability for the first time. The platform's content strategy expanded with exclusive live sports, including NFL Sunday Night Football, Premier League matches, and original programming like Poker Face and Mrs. Davis.

2025

The Future Unfolds

NBCUniversal continues to shape global media with streaming innovation and potential Sky restructuring.

As of 2025, Comcast's NBCUniversal empire spans broadcast TV (NBC), cable networks (USA, Bravo, MSNBC, CNBC), film (Universal Pictures, DreamWorks, Illumination, Focus Features), streaming (Peacock), theme parks, and European operations via Sky. Discussions about potentially spinning off cable networks reflect the ongoing transformation of the media landscape in the streaming era.
